Why I taught a computer to see stairs

My first real AI project started with my grandma. She has trouble seeing, and she fell down the stairs because she couldn't tell where the next step was. It scared me a lot, and I kept thinking: what if something could just tell her the stairs are there?

So the summer after 9th grade, I tried to build that.

The setup

My Jetson Nano. Tiny computer, a lot of cables.

I used an NVIDIA Jetson Nano, which is basically a small computer made for running AI models. Instead of training a model from nothing, I used transfer learning on SSD-MobileNet. That means you take a model that already knows how to find objects and retrain it on your own pictures, which in my case were a LOT of pictures of stairs.

After training, I exported the model to ONNX so it could run on the Jetson, and then it could detect stairs in real time through the camera.

It works!! The green box is the model finding the stairs.

What I learned

  • How machine learning models actually get trained (it's a lot of data prep).
  • How to use Git, which I honestly did not understand before this.
  • Docker, and why everyone complains about it.
  • That the "why" behind a project keeps you going when things break.

I ended up earning the NVIDIA Deep Learning Institute certificate in AI and ML through this project, and it's still the project I'm proudest of. If you want to see the code, it's on my GitHub.
